Investment Access Routes

Select India or GIFT City to view only the routes relevant to that market framework.

The investor opens or uses the required banking, trading and demat arrangements and makes investment decisions directly.

  • May involve: NRE/NRO bank, broker and depository participant.
  • Common access: listed shares, eligible exchange-traded products and other broker-supported instruments.
  • Investor controls: security selection and transaction instructions.
Useful when: the investor wants direct control and understands the operational account structure.

A SEBI-registered portfolio manager manages a portfolio under the selected discretionary or non-discretionary arrangement.

  • May involve: portfolio manager, bank, broker, demat participant and sometimes custodian.
  • Key dependency: the manager may support defined banking, brokerage or custody arrangements.
  • Investor decision: provider, strategy and mandate—not each transaction in a discretionary PMS.
Useful when: the investor prefers a separately managed portfolio and meets applicable provider requirements.

The investor subscribes to units of a pooled investment vehicle managed according to the fund's stated strategy and documents.

  • May involve: AIF manager, trustee, custodian, banker, registrar or administrator.
  • Investor reviews: category, strategy, tenure, drawdowns, liquidity, fees and risk disclosures.
  • Account structure: differs from direct equity or PMS onboarding.
Useful when: the investor seeks access to a pooled alternative strategy and accepts its investment terms.

Eligible foreign entities and institutional investors access Indian markets through an FPI-oriented registration and custody framework.

  • May involve: designated depository participant, custodian, bank, broker and tax advisers.
  • Higher complexity: entity classification, ownership, control, KYC and regulatory documentation.
  • Not generally the retail NRI route.
Useful when: an overseas fund, institution, family structure or eligible entity requires institutional market access.

A person who is neither an Indian citizen nor an OCI may require a foreign-investor pathway rather than the NRI route to access Indian securities.

  • Possible route: FPI-oriented access, a permitted fund or managed product, or another route supported by the relevant provider.
  • May involve: designated depository participant, custodian, designated bank, broker and tax advisers.
  • Provider checks: home jurisdiction, sanctions screening, tax status, source of wealth/funds and product acceptance.
Useful when: a foreign citizen wants exposure to Indian securities without NRI/OCI status. Final route and eligibility must be confirmed by the regulated provider.

An overseas company, fund, trust, partnership, foundation or other legal arrangement may seek portfolio access through the FPI framework.

  • May involve: designated depository participant, custodian, designated bank, broker, legal counsel and tax adviser.
  • Entity due diligence: constitutional documents, registration evidence, ownership/control chain, beneficial owners, authorised persons and regulatory status.
  • Ongoing obligations: disclosures, change reporting, tax and regulatory reporting may apply.
Useful when: the entity wants portfolio exposure to listed or otherwise permitted Indian securities.

A person resident outside India invests in the capital instruments of an Indian company, or in another permitted Indian entity or structure, under the applicable foreign-investment framework.

  • May involve: Indian investee, authorised dealer bank, legal counsel, company secretary, valuation and tax professionals.
  • Key checks: investor and beneficial-owner eligibility, sectoral conditions, automatic or government approval route, pricing, instruments, approvals and FEMA reporting.
  • Nature of route: ownership-oriented investment in an Indian business, rather than exchange-based portfolio investing.
Useful when: the investor proposes to acquire, subscribe to or hold a strategic or ownership interest in an Indian business.

An eligible overseas investment vehicle registered with SEBI as an FVCI invests through the specialised venture-capital framework in permitted Indian venture-capital investments.

  • May involve: SEBI registration, domestic custodian, designated bank, Indian venture-capital undertaking or eligible fund, legal and tax advisers.
  • Investor checks: overseas legal form, regulatory or tax status, fit-and-proper assessment, investment strategy and registration eligibility.
  • Important distinction: FVCI is a specialised registered investor route and should not be treated as the same as FPI or ordinary FDI.
Useful when: an eligible foreign fund or investment vehicle intends to make venture-capital investments under the FVCI framework.

An NRI or OCI invests in eligible Indian securities, entities or investment vehicles on a non-repatriation basis using permitted funds and an appropriate NRO-linked arrangement.

  • May include eligible listed securities, capital instruments, LLP interests, investment-vehicle units and qualifying start-up convertible notes.
  • Sale or maturity proceeds are generally credited to an NRO account and are not freely repatriable.
  • Provider checks include investor status, instrument eligibility, payment route, KYC, tax and transaction documentation.
Useful when: an NRI/OCI is comfortable investing on a non-repatriation basis or using Indian-source funds.

A person resident outside India subscribes to units of an eligible Indian investment vehicle, such as an AIF, REIT or InvIT, under the applicable vehicle and investor-eligibility framework.

  • May involve a manager, trustee, sponsor, custodian, registrar/administrator, banker and tax advisers.
  • Key checks include vehicle type, investor category, minimum commitment, beneficial ownership, payment route, liquidity and distribution terms.
  • This is the cross-border regulatory route into the vehicle; the AIF, REIT or InvIT is the underlying product or structure.
Useful when: a foreign individual or entity wants pooled or unit-based exposure to Indian assets.

An eligible foreign investor accesses permitted Indian government, corporate or other debt instruments through the applicable FPI, NRI/OCI or instrument-specific framework.

  • May include government securities, treasury bills, corporate bonds, non-convertible debentures, commercial paper and other permitted debt instruments.
  • May involve a DDP/custodian, designated bank, broker or debt platform, issuer, depository and tax advisers.
  • Key checks include investor category, instrument eligibility, investment limits, settlement, withholding, maturity and repatriation treatment.
Useful when: the investor seeks Indian fixed-income or private-debt exposure rather than equity ownership.

The investor accesses products or services offered through entities regulated in India's International Financial Services Centre.

  • May involve: IFSC bank, broker, fund manager, portfolio manager or custodian.
  • Important distinction: IFSC products and account structures are separate from India domestic products.
  • Currency and eligibility: depend on the provider, product and applicable IFSC framework.
Useful when: the investor is considering internationally structured or foreign-currency opportunities through GIFT City.

A foreign individual may explore funds, portfolio services, brokerage or other products offered by IFSCA-regulated entities in GIFT IFSC.

  • May involve: IFSC bank, broker, fund manager, portfolio manager, custodian or distributor.
  • Account structure: generally product- and provider-specific and may use foreign-currency arrangements.
  • Checks: investor category, country acceptance, accredited-investor conditions where applicable and KYC/tax documentation.
Useful when: a foreign investor is considering internationally structured products through GIFT City.

Foreign companies, funds, family structures and institutions may access eligible IFSC funds, portfolio services, capital markets, banking and custody offerings.

  • May involve: IFSC fund manager, broker, exchange member, bank, custodian, trustee or administrator.
  • Entity checks: legal form, jurisdiction, regulatory status, ownership/control, beneficial ownership and authorised signatories.
  • Product checks: minimum commitment, currency, liquidity, leverage, investor-category and placement conditions.
Useful when: an overseas entity wants internationally structured exposure through an IFSCA-regulated ecosystem.

An adviser, external asset manager or wealth manager helps the investor evaluate options and coordinate providers.

  • May sit across: direct investing, PMS, AIF and GIFT City journeys.
  • Role: advice or coordination depends on the participant's licence and engagement.
  • Execution and custody: remain with the relevant regulated providers.
Useful when: the investor wants support understanding choices or coordinating a multi-provider journey.
GIFT City and GIFT IFSC are not the same as India’s domestic financial market. GIFT IFSC is India’s international financial services centre and is regulated by the International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA). Products, legal entities, account structures, currencies, eligibility rules and investor protections must be checked for the specific IFSC offering.
The basic concept

What does investing through GIFT City mean?

It generally means investing in, or using financial services offered by, an entity established in GIFT IFSC. Depending on the investor and offering, this may include an IFSC fund, portfolio management service, brokerage or exchange access, banking, custody, insurance or another IFSCA-regulated financial service.

Separate regulated entityAn Indian financial group may have both a domestic entity and an IFSC entity. The investor should identify the exact legal entity and its IFSCA authorisation.
International orientationOfferings may be structured for cross-border investors, foreign-currency investment and international assets or strategies, subject to the provider and product framework.
Product-specific accessEligibility does not arise merely because an investor is an NRI, OCI, resident Indian or foreign investor. Each scheme or service applies its own permitted-investor and onboarding criteria.
Different from domestic IndiaAn IFSC fund, PMS account or brokerage arrangement should not be treated as the same product as a domestic AIF, PMS, mutual fund or broking account.
Possible access routes

How investors may access the GIFT IFSC ecosystem

The investor subscribes to units or interests in a scheme managed by an IFSCA-registered Fund Management Entity.

  • May include retail, restricted, venture-capital or other permitted schemes.
  • Review the offer document or placement memorandum, strategy, currency, commitment, fees, liquidity and valuation terms.

An eligible investor appoints an IFSC Fund Management Entity to manage or advise a segregated portfolio under a portfolio-management agreement.

  • Confirm minimum eligibility, mandate, custody, banking and asset universe.
  • Understand discretionary versus non-discretionary authority and reporting.

An investor may obtain access to products supported by an IFSCA-regulated broker, exchange member, bank and custodian.

  • The available securities and derivatives depend on the provider and exchange framework.
  • Account, margin, settlement and custody requirements must be confirmed.

An IFSC Banking Unit may support permitted foreign-currency accounts, remittances, deposits, financing or investment-related cash flows.

  • The account type and permitted use depend on investor status and transaction purpose.
  • Funding should follow the remittance and foreign-exchange rules applicable to the investor.

A custodian, administrator, trustee, registrar or other fiduciary may support safekeeping, settlement, records, valuation and investor reporting.

  • The exact ecosystem differs for a fund, PMS mandate and brokerage account.
  • Identify who holds the assets and who maintains the investor records.

A suitably authorised adviser, distributor or wealth manager may help the investor evaluate and access an IFSC offering.

  • Confirm the participant’s licence, role and compensation.
  • Facilitation, advice, distribution, execution and custody are different functions.
Who may consider it

Investor categories and key questions

May consider IFSC funds, portfolio services, banking or market products, subject to the scheme and provider accepting the investor’s country of residence and status.

  • Is the investment funded from overseas or from India?
  • What is the permitted remittance route?
  • What are the tax and reporting implications in both jurisdictions?

May use an IFSC access route for internationally structured products without assuming that the domestic NRI or India retail route applies.

  • Is the home jurisdiction accepted?
  • Are beneficial ownership, sanctions and source-of-wealth checks satisfied?
  • Is the investor eligible for the specific retail or non-retail offering?

Access depends on the product and the remittance or investment framework available to a resident. Availability should not be assumed solely because the product is located in India.

  • Is resident participation permitted?
  • Does the investment use the Liberalised Remittance Scheme or another permitted route?
  • What limits, declarations and tax reporting apply?

May consider funds, portfolio services, family-investment arrangements or other offerings, subject to legal-form, ownership, control and regulatory-status review.

  • Who is the investing legal person?
  • Who are the beneficial owners and authorised signatories?
  • Are constitutional, tax and regulatory documents current?
Typical journey

From interest to investment

  1. Define the objective: fund investment, managed portfolio, brokerage access, banking or another service.
  2. Identify the exact IFSC entity: verify the legal name and IFSCA registration or authorisation.
  3. Check investor eligibility: residence, legal status, country acceptance, investor category and minimum commitment.
  4. Review the offering: strategy, assets, currency, risk, fees, liquidity, leverage, valuation, conflicts and exit terms.
  5. Confirm the operating structure: bank, custodian, broker, administrator, trustee and reporting parties.
  6. Complete KYC and tax documentation: identity, address, tax residence, FATCA/CRS, source of funds or wealth and beneficial ownership as applicable.
  7. Fund through the permitted route: follow the provider’s banking instructions and the investor’s applicable foreign-exchange rules.
  8. Retain records and monitor: statements, valuations, distributions, tax documents, material changes and grievance channels.
Before appointing a provider

Questions an investor should ask

Regulatory identity
  • What is the exact legal entity?
  • What activity is it authorised to perform?
  • Can the registration be verified in the IFSCA directory?
Investment structure
  • What am I legally acquiring?
  • Who owns and holds the underlying assets?
  • Is the offering open-ended, closed-ended or mandate-based?
Economics and liquidity
  • What are all management, performance, custody and transaction fees?
  • When and how can I redeem or exit?
  • Can withdrawals be gated, suspended or delayed?
Currency and tax
  • What is the subscription and reporting currency?
  • Who bears currency risk?
  • What tax, withholding and home-country reporting may apply?
Risk and governance
  • What are the principal investment and counterparty risks?
  • Who values the portfolio?
  • What conflicts, leverage or related-party transactions are permitted?
Service and protection
  • Who handles complaints and investor servicing?
  • How often are reports issued?
  • What happens if the manager, broker or custodian changes?

For NRI / OCI investors using India direct investing

What is a PIS account?

A Portfolio Investment Scheme (PIS) arrangement is a designated banking mechanism used for certain NRI/OCI purchases and sales of listed Indian equity on a repatriation basis through a recognised stock exchange. The designated bank monitors eligible transactions and applicable holding limits.

NRE (PIS): repatriation basis
  • Funded by inward remittance or eligible NRE funds.
  • Used exclusively for transactions permitted under the applicable repatriation route.
  • Net sale proceeds may generally be remitted outside India, subject to taxes and compliance.
  • Useful when repatriability of eligible investment proceeds is important.
NRO account: non-repatriation basis
  • Used for investments on a non-repatriation basis and for Indian income or other permitted NRO funds.
  • “NRO PIS” is largely legacy terminology; existing NRO (PIS) accounts were redesignated as ordinary NRO accounts.
  • Repatriation from NRO is subject to applicable FEMA, tax and banking conditions.
  • The bank or broker should confirm the exact account linkage required for the chosen product.
Important: A PIS arrangement is not required for every NRI/OCI investment. Mutual funds, AIFs, PMS, GIFT City products and other instruments can follow different payment and account rules. The relevant bank, broker or manager must confirm whether PIS is required for the selected route.
Separate PAN application guide

Obtain or verify PAN before Indian investment-account opening

A valid PAN is generally required before Resident Indian, NRI/OCI and Indian entity securities onboarding can be completed. Investors may continue learning and preparing documents, but the flow should not represent brokerage, demat, PMS, AIF or other Indian investment accounts as activated until PAN has been allotted and verified.

Resident Indian individual eligible for Instant e-PAN
  1. Use the Income Tax Department's free Instant e-PAN service.
  2. You must be an individual who has never been allotted PAN.
  3. A valid Aadhaar and Aadhaar-linked mobile number are required.
  4. The service issues a digitally signed e-PAN; a physical card is not required for PAN validity.

NRI / OCI, applicant without eligible Aadhaar, or legal entity
  1. Use an authorised PAN service provider's new-PAN application service.
  2. Select the applicant category shown by the provider and follow the current application form and document instructions.
  3. Overseas applicants may need prescribed certification, attestation or physical-document dispatch.
  4. Keep the acknowledgement number and track allotment before completing investment-account opening.

Apply through Protean PAN Services ↗

The authorised provider may update forms, evidence requirements and fees. Follow the instructions displayed on the official application site.

Foreign Portfolio Investor route
  • The appointed Designated Depository Participant/custodian should coordinate PAN with the integrated FPI registration, KYC, bank, custody and demat process.
  • The applicant should follow the custodian's document, certification and application instructions.
  • A retail applicant should not independently choose an FPI PAN process without the DDP/custodian.
Check before applying
  • Do not apply for a second PAN.
  • Check whether PAN has already been allotted and whether it is active.
  • Use matching names and core particulars across passport, Aadhaar, incorporation and investment documents.
  • Resolve discrepancies or use the authorised correction process before account activation.

Verify PAN on Income Tax portal ↗

Do not create a second PAN. Possession of more than one PAN can attract consequences. Where PAN may already exist, verify it first and use the authorised correction or reprint process instead of making a new application.
