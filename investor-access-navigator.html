Tax awareness before selecting an investment route

Tax can affect documentation, withholding, return filing, remittance and the net amount received by an investor. This guide identifies questions to discuss with a qualified tax adviser; it does not calculate tax or determine treaty eligibility.

Tax residence is separate from FEMA status. An investor's banking or investment classification does not by itself determine Indian tax residence. Tax residence must be assessed separately for each tax year, including any resident, non-resident, deemed-resident or not-ordinarily-resident conditions that may apply.
Types of investment income
  • Interest and dividends.
  • Short-term or long-term capital gains.
  • PMS portfolio income and realised gains.
  • AIF or fund distributions.
  • Income or gains from unlisted and strategic investments.
The character and treatment of income depend on the product, transaction and applicable law.
Withholding and return filing
  • Tax may be deducted before interest, distributions or sale proceeds are paid.
  • Tax deducted at source may not equal the investor's final liability.
  • An Indian tax return may be required to report gains, claim credit or seek a refund.
  • PAN and matching tax records may affect withholding and reporting.
Tax treaty documentation
  • A DTAA may affect the taxation of specified income.
  • Eligibility is not automatic and depends on the investor and transaction.
  • A Tax Residency Certificate and prescribed declarations or forms may be required.
  • The investor's home country may also require income or asset reporting.
Repatriation is a separate process
  • Payment of Indian tax does not by itself make proceeds freely repatriable.
  • The bank may require tax-payment evidence and remittance documentation.
  • Form 15CA and, where applicable, a chartered accountant's Form 15CB may be relevant for a remittance.
  • FEMA, banking and tax requirements should be checked together.

GIFT City / IFSC Investor residence, product structure, applicable IFSC provisions, currency and home-country reporting.

Why this matters: Compliance is not only about opening the account. Investors may need to respond to KYC refreshes, FATCA/CRS confirmations, change reporting requests and periodic statements throughout the relationship.
Getting started

Initial compliance information

Before a provider activates an account or accepts an investment, it may request enough information to identify the investor, understand the route and perform its legal and regulatory checks.

  • Identity and address evidence, PAN and tax-residency information.
  • FATCA/CRS declarations, source-of-funds or source-of-wealth information and bank-account evidence.
  • For entities, constitutional documents, authority documents, beneficial owners and controlling persons.
Keep information current

Ongoing KYC and change reporting

Investors may need to keep information current after onboarding and report specified changes to the bank, broker, fund manager, custodian or other provider.

  • Common updates include address, email, mobile number, tax residence, bank account or residency status.
  • Legal entities may need to report changes in directors, partners, trustees, authorised signatories, ownership or control.
  • Providers may also perform periodic KYC refreshes and ask for updated declarations or documents.
What you may receive

Reports and statements

Different participants may send different reports. The investor should understand who issues each document and review them promptly.

  • Examples include contract notes, account statements, demat or custody statements, portfolio reports, tax statements and capital-call or distribution notices.
  • A broker, DP or custodian may report holdings and transactions, while a portfolio or fund manager may send valuation, strategy and investor reports.
  • Corporate-action notices and KYC refresh requests may also form part of the reporting cycle.
Investor responsibilities

What the investor is expected to do

The investor typically remains responsible for providing accurate information and for responding promptly when the provider requests confirmations or updates.

  • Review statements and confirmations and report discrepancies or unauthorised activity quickly.
  • Keep bank details, contact details and tax information current.
  • Retain transaction, remittance and tax records for future reporting, audits or remittance requirements.
Route-specific focus

Compliance topics vary by investment route

The route affects what must be monitored and reported over time.

  • India direct / NRI: account status, permitted bank route, demat setup, repatriation basis and updated KYC.
  • PMS / AIF: mandate or scheme documents, statements, drawdowns or distributions and investor communications.
  • FPI / FDI / FVCI / IFSC routes: ownership and control disclosures, route-specific registration or approvals, transaction reporting and material-change reporting.
  • NRI/OCI non-repatriation: continued status, NRO-linked payment records, instrument eligibility and tax/remittance evidence.
  • Investment Vehicles / foreign debt: investor-category confirmations, beneficial-ownership updates, limits, distributions or interest, maturity and route-specific reporting.
Entities

Additional requirements for legal entities

Companies, LLPs, trusts, funds and family structures often have additional documentation and reporting needs beyond those of individual investors.

  • Authority evidence such as board, partner or trustee resolutions and authorised-signatory details.
  • Ownership and control charts, ultimate beneficial owners and controlling persons.
  • Entity documents may need periodic refresh and changes in ownership, signatories or regulatory status may need prompt reporting.
Roles and accountability

Provider obligations versus investor obligations

The Service Provider may collect information from the investor and then complete its own reporting to SEBI, RBI, IFSCA, depositories, exchanges, tax authorities or other bodies.

  • Facilon can help structure information, reminders, status tracking and evidence.
  • The regulated bank, broker, manager, custodian or other provider remains responsible for determining and completing its regulatory filings.
  • The investor should still confirm what information must be provided, updated or reviewed on an ongoing basis.
Practical takeaway

Questions to ask before you proceed

Before choosing a provider or route, ask what will be required not only at onboarding but throughout the relationship.

  • What changes must be reported and within what timeframe?
  • What statements or reports will be provided and by whom?
  • Will you need support tracking periodic compliance requests, reporting deadlines or document refresh cycles?

Before choosing a provider: eligibility to open an account does not mean that a product is suitable. Review the risk, cost, authority, liquidity and protections that apply to the relationship.
Risk & liquidity

Can you tolerate loss or delay?

Different products carry different levels of market, currency, concentration, leverage and liquidity risk.

  • Check lock-in periods, redemption windows, withdrawal conditions and exit charges.
  • Understand valuation frequency and whether the investment can be sold quickly.
  • Do not assume that past performance or a target return is guaranteed.
Fees & economics

Understand the complete cost

Several participants may charge separately, and fees can materially reduce net returns.

  • Review account, brokerage, custody, management, performance, administration and exit charges.
  • Ask whether a distributor, adviser or facilitator receives a commission or referral fee.
  • Request a clear illustration of upfront, recurring, performance-linked and exit costs.
Provider verification

Know who is doing what

Confirm the provider's regulated role before relying on its advice, execution or custody services.

  • Check whether the participant is acting as adviser, distributor, broker, manager, custodian or facilitator.
  • Verify registration details and the legal entity receiving funds or documents.
  • Understand related-party arrangements and possible conflicts of interest.
Rights & complaints

Know how to escalate a problem

Investors should understand how to raise complaints about delays, unauthorised transactions, reporting errors or fees.

  • Start with the Service Provider and its grievance or compliance officer.
  • Escalate through the applicable regulatory or market-infrastructure mechanism where necessary.
  • Retain account records, correspondence, statements and complaint references.
Fraud & digital safety

Protect access and payments

Investment journeys involve sensitive identity, bank and transaction information.

  • Never share passwords, PINs or OTPs.
  • Do not transfer money to personal or unverified accounts.
  • Be cautious of guaranteed returns, urgent pressure and unsolicited links or payment instructions.
Instructions & actions

Understand who can act

After onboarding, different arrangements determine who may place instructions and how corporate actions are handled.

  • Review registered email and mobile requirements, mandates and powers of attorney.
  • Understand discretionary versus non-discretionary authority.
  • Know how dividends, rights issues, buybacks, voting, capital calls and other actions will be communicated.
Nomination & succession

Plan for death or incapacity

Nomination and succession arrangements may differ across bank, demat, PMS, fund and custody accounts.

  • Understand the distinction between a nominee and a legal heir.
  • Keep nominee, joint-holder and authorised-person details current.
  • For legal entities, maintain successor trustees, directors or authorised representatives where relevant.
Privacy & consent

Know how documents are used

Investors should understand why information is collected and which participants may receive it.

  • Check the purpose of collection, sharing, retention and correction.
  • Understand when consent may be withdrawn and when regulated retention may still apply.
  • Report suspected misuse or unauthorised access promptly.
